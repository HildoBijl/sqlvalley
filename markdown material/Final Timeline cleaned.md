# Phase 1 — The Startup

## Event 1 — 18 January 2023: Two Friends and a Vision

Caesar and Bob present their business idea during a Digital Markets seminar. Their pitch frames art as “a public good trapped in private markets.”

- The project receives the highest grade in the seminar.
- Students vote it the seminar’s most innovative idea.
- The university newspaper publishes an article about the project.

## Event 2 — 9 February 2023: First Proof-of-Concept Prototype

Caesar and Bob complete the first working prototype after approximately three weeks of development.

The prototype includes:
- A basic frontend.
- Mock user and asset data.
- A simulated fractional-ownership system.
- Early versions of the `accounts`, `products`, `transactions`, and `shareholders` tables.

Caesar and Bob add themselves, family members, and friends as mock users. Although the implementation is basic and inconsistent, the prototype demonstrates that the concept could become a functioning platform.

**Accounts milestone:** 15 prototype accounts.

## Event 3 — 3 March 2023: Student Startup Night

Caesar and Bob present the prototype at Student Startup Night.

The presentation attracts significant interest, although many attendees compare the platform to NFTs. Caesar and Bob emphasize that HeriShare uses a centralized platform tied to identifiable physical assets rather than a decentralized blockchain system.

Following the presentation, the platform is opened for expressions of interest and early registrations.

**Accounts milestone:** 30 new accounts.

## Event 4 — 11 April 2023: First Museum Partnership

HeriShare enters its first formal partnership with the **Northbridge Museum of Modern Craft & Design**, a small regional museum operating from a former textile warehouse.

The museum focuses on post-war craft, industrial design, furniture, ceramics, textiles, and local design history. Because of serious funding difficulties, it agrees to test HeriShare’s unconventional restoration-financing model.

The museum selects five objects requiring restoration:

1. **The Orlov Modular Chair Series, 1974**
2. **“Signal Loom,” 1982**
3. **The Van Acker Ceramic Prototype Set, 1968**
4. **The Luma-3 Exhibition Lamp, 1979**
5. **“Blue Factory Window,” 1934**

An art critic affiliated with the museum estimates:

- Each object’s current value.
- Restoration costs.
- Expected value following restoration.

HeriShare incorporates restoration, handling, and platform costs into the price of the fractional shares.

The museum describes the partnership as:

> “A new model for public participation in cultural preservation.”

HeriShare markets it as:

> “Opening cultural ownership to everyone while saving endangered objects from storage and decay.”

The partnership attracts art enthusiasts, preservation supporters, and speculative investors.

**Accounts milestone:** 35 new accounts.

## Event 5 — 6 May 2023: Platform V1.0 Launch

HeriShare integrates Stripe and officially launches the first public version of its platform.

A promotional campaign attracts a substantial wave of new users. The founders also begin contacting additional museums, cultural centres, botanical gardens, estates, and other organisations holding culturally significant assets.

**Accounts milestone:** 95 new accounts.

## Event 6 — 2 June 2023: Official Company Registration

HeriShare is formally registered as **HeriShare Inc.**

The company adopts initial Terms of Service based largely on adapted equity-crowdfunding templates. Its legal and operational structure remains immature.

# Phase 2 — The Accelerator

## Event 7 — 20 June 2023: Entry into the Accelerator

HeriShare is accepted into a Silicon Valley startup accelerator.

The accelerator selects HeriShare because of:

- Its cultural-preservation mission.
- Its potential as a fintech and marketplace platform.
- Caesar’s ability to promote the company.
- Bob’s technical expertise.
- The emotional appeal of “saving culture from private hoarding.”
- Early evidence that users are willing to fund real assets.

At this stage, the company still consists mainly of a polished demonstration, a small user base, and a limited number of real assets.

The accelerator provides:

- Office space.
- Mentorship.
- Legal and fintech workshops.
- Investor introductions.
- Business-development support.

The accelerator positions HeriShare as a combination of fintech, cultural preservation, and marketplace infrastructure.

## Event 8 — 23 June 2023: Seed Funding

HeriShare raises **$150,000** in seed funding in exchange for equity.

The funding is intended for:

- Legal and regulatory work.
- Platform development.
- Marketing.
- Initial hiring.
- Asset acquisition and onboarding.

The investors place significant pressure on the founders to prioritise scalability and rapid growth.

## Event 9 — 28 June 2023: First Mentor Review

During the first formal accelerator review, mentors identify two urgent weaknesses:
- The transaction infrastructure is unreliable.
- The company has no consistent process for onboarding and evaluating assets.

The founders are instructed to formalise both systems before expanding further.

## Event 10 — 3 July 2023: First Team Expansion

HeriShare hires its first employees.

Initial roles include:
- **Operations Coordinator:** Manages relationships with asset owners, logistics, insurance, restoration providers, and condition reports.
- **Customer Support and Community Associate:** Handles questions concerning accounts, payments, purchases, and the platform.

Caesar is formally entered into the employee system as part of the operations function. Paris joins in customer support and community management.

## Event 11 — 18 July 2023: Second Listing Sells Out

A privately owned contemporary sculpture is fully funded within 72 hours.

The successful listing provides stronger evidence that HeriShare can attract users beyond the museum-restoration project.

## Event 12 — 25 August 2023: Major Partnership Expansion

HeriShare signs several museums, estates, and cultural organisations to its restoration programme.

New listings include:
- Contemporary paintings from emerging artists.
- Mid-tier antiquities with documented provenance.
- Privately owned sculptures.
- Historical documents and letters.
- Film and cultural memorabilia.
- Objects held by struggling specialist museums.

Several financially distressed institutions join the platform hoping that fractional investment will provide a new source of funding and public attention.

## Event 13 — 1 September 2023: Accelerator Demo Day

HeriShare presents its progress to investors, mentors, and potential commercial partners during the accelerator’s Demo Day.

The presentation focuses on:
- Successful early listings.
- User growth.
- The platform’s cultural-preservation mission.
- The possibility of building a scalable secondary marketplace.

## Event 14 — 5 October 2023: Internal Marketplace Launch

HeriShare releases a major platform update introducing an internal secondary marketplace.

Users can now sell their shares to other registered users instead of waiting for the underlying asset to be sold.

The update substantially increases transaction volume and transforms HeriShare from a restoration-funding platform into a more active speculative marketplace.

## Event 15 — 19 November 2023: Viral Press Coverage

A technology blog publishes a viral article describing HeriShare as:

> “The Robinhood of Art Ownership.”

The article creates a major wave of public attention.

The user base grows from approximately **300 accounts to 750 accounts in ten days**.

Public figures, celebrities, speculative investors, and inexperienced retail users join the platform.

**Accounts milestone:** Approximately 450 new accounts. Later organic growth, the May 2024 scandal, migrated client records and post-pivot applications bring the final 30 April 2025 snapshot to 1,500 accounts, as specified in `accounts timeline cleaned.md`.

The sudden growth exposes weaknesses in:
- Account management.
- Transaction reliability.
- Payment processing.
- The ownership ledger.
- Platform security.
- Customer support.
- Asset logistics.

HeriShare begins expanding its engineering, operations, acquisition, legal, finance, and marketing teams.

# Phase 3 — The Loophole

## Event 16 — 21 April 2024: First Majority Ownership Accumulation

A billionaire uses several accounts to quietly acquire a combined **51% ownership interest** in a listed sculpture.

The platform evaluates ownership separately for each account and does not detect that the accounts are controlled by the same individual.

This creates the first known instance of a single beneficial owner obtaining majority control over an asset.

## Event 17 — 23 April 2024: The Loophole Spreads

Other wealthy users begin exploiting the same weakness.

Through multiple accounts and coordinated purchases, a small number of individuals accumulate majority ownership positions in several listed assets.

The activity initially goes unnoticed because the database does not reliably connect related accounts or identify common beneficial owners.

## Event 18 — 2 May 2024: Relocation Request

A majority shareholder requests that an asset be removed from its museum and relocated to the shareholder’s private residence.

The request causes internal confusion.

The legal team discovers that the platform agreements did not adequately address:
- Majority ownership.
- Physical possession.
- Asset relocation.
- Voting rights.
- Control by related accounts.
- Conflicts between shareholders and cultural institutions.

The company cannot immediately reject the request because its contracts and platform rules were built around the assumption that ownership would remain widely distributed.

## Event 19 — 11 May 2024: Media Leak

An article exposes the ownership loophole under the headline:

> “Fractional Ownership of Art or Billionaire Scheme?”

The scandal generates intense public attention and hundreds of new registrations in a single day.

Many users create:
- Unverified accounts.
- Duplicate accounts.
- Accounts using incomplete or false information.
- Multiple accounts connected to the same person.
- Accounts intended to imitate the strategy used by wealthy investors.

Museums and NGOs suspend their collaborations and threaten legal action.

## Event 20 — 28 May 2024: Platform Destabilisation

Approximately 40% of listed assets are now effectively controlled by individual majority shareholders.

HeriShare has no reliable contractual or technical mechanism to reverse the concentration of ownership.

Bob wants to suspend the platform immediately. Caesar argues that the company should remain operational while management searches for a solution.

Attempts to halt trading trigger legal objections from majority owners.

At the same time:
- Transaction revenue rises sharply.
- Legal and advisory costs increase.
- Platform activity becomes unstable.
- Fake and duplicate accounts distort account records.
- New registrations are disabled.
- The platform is temporarily taken offline.
- Many suspicious accounts show misleading or abnormal `last_login_at` values.

## Event 21 — 9 June 2024: Internal Breakdown

HeriShare takes the platform fully offline.

The crisis results in:

- Layoffs.
- Employee resignations.
- Salary reductions.
- Salary freezes.
- Contract changes.
- Internal conflict between the founders.
- Loss of institutional partners.
- Major legal and operational costs.

# Phase 4 — The Pivot

## Event 22 — 25 August 2024: Board Intervention

The board holds an emergency meeting to determine the company’s future.

The board votes to abandon HeriShare’s original public-access mission and rebuild the company as a private marketplace for valuable and culturally significant assets.

The vote is nearly unanimous. Bob abstains because he opposes the new direction but considers himself partly responsible for the platform’s failure.

## Event 23 — 3 September 2024: Marcelle Johnson Is Hired

Marcelle Johnson is hired to repair the company’s disorganised database and adapt it to the new business model.

Her work includes:
- Cleaning account records.
- Identifying duplicate and incomplete data.
- Merging client information into the `accounts` table.
- Preserving compatibility with old transactions.
- Repurposing deprecated structures.
- Correcting inconsistent formats.
- Reconstructing relationships between users, clients, products, and transactions.

The imported client data is significantly messier than the original account data because it was collected in different formats and for different purposes.

## Event 24 — 29 September 2024: Quiet Rebranding

HeriShare quietly relaunches as an **“Exclusive Asset Marketplace.”**

Registrations reopen, but signup volume is much lower than before the scandal. The proportion of incomplete and unverified accounts remains relatively high.

The new business model is communicated to former museum and NGO partners. Approximately 90% leave the platform.

The remaining institutional partners are mostly organisations under serious financial pressure.

Private sellers become the dominant source of listings, including:
- Auction houses.
- Estate representatives.
- Private collectors.
- Rare-item dealers.
- Individuals attempting to sell objects of questionable cultural or financial value.
    

## Event 25 — October 2024 to April 2025: Recovery Phase

Over the following six months, the company gradually stabilises around its new marketplace model.

During this period:
- Revenue returns to positive levels.
- New employees are hired.
- Departments are reorganised.
- Employment contracts are replaced or amended.
- Database-cleaning work continues.
- Old and new business records remain mixed together.
- Deprecated tables and fields are retained because existing systems still depend on them.

Marcelle becomes central to the company’s technical and operational recovery.

# Phase 5 — The Story

## Event 26 — Marcelle’s Sabbatical

After approximately six months of repairing the company’s systems, Marcelle begins an extended sabbatical.
Before leaving, she announces plans to improve internal monitoring after her return.

The intended system would:
- Track employee activity.
- Strengthen internal security.
- Identify inefficient workflows.
- Help investigate suspicious database activity.
- Distinguish legitimate work from unauthorised changes.

Marcelle has access to limited work-presence or login-time information that will become available to the player in the final module. The eventual table is provisionally called `checkInOut`; its exact name, schema and collection mechanism are not yet fixed.

The prototype is difficult to use and only collects partial information. Marcelle is initially the only person with access to it.

## Event 27 — The Player Joins HeriShare

The player joins HeriShare as a data engineering intern while Marcelle is absent.

Marcelle was supposed to manage and onboard the player. Her sabbatical leaves the onboarding incomplete, so the player has no row in the company database and cannot reply to internal chat messages for most of the story.

Because the database contains a mixture of:
- Current data.
- Deprecated structures.
- Duplicate accounts.
- Historical transactions.
- Incorrect records.
- Deliberate manipulation.

Management struggles to assign the player a normal task.

Caesar asks the player to investigate rumours that someone inside the company is manipulating the database.

Caesar does not initially believe there is a serious internal threat. He assigns the investigation to a junior employee partly to keep them occupied and avoid committing to a formal audit.

## Event 28 — Internal Suspicion Escalates

Several employees take advantage of the company’s disorder and Marcelle’s absence.

- **Bob** considers sabotaging the company he helped build. He believes Marcelle’s return would prevent him from carrying out his plan.
- **Paris** attempts to damage Marcelle’s reputation.
- **Wheatley** gathers information without clearly revealing his purpose.
- **Elvis** begins financial misconduct involving questionable expenses and approvals. His exact scheme and personal motive remain to be finalized.

These are the only four malicious actors in the story. Ordinary mistakes and legitimate historical inconsistencies may still exist, but no other employee is another mole.

The combined activity creates the impression that a single “Mole” is responsible, even though several people are independently manipulating the database.

## Event 29 — Marcelle’s Monitoring Data Becomes Available

Near the end of the story, Marcelle returns or re-establishes contact and the player gains access to a new table, provisionally called `checkInOut`, containing work-presence or login-time information.

Marcelle has stronger technical knowledge than the player but lacks the context the player has collected through conversations, database investigations, and earlier exercises.

The table alone does not identify a mole. The player must combine it with:

- Account history.
- Employee records.
- Contracts.
- Transactions.
- Product ownership.
- Expenses.
- Performance data.
- Work-presence or login-time records.
- Context gathered from other characters.

The final module contains three multi-layered exercises. Each combines the new table with evidence established by earlier investigations and returns the single responsible insider for that case: Bob, Paris or Elvis. Wheatley does not appear in the database and is addressed only in the post-completion dialogue. The precise table schema and reveal queries remain to be designed.
