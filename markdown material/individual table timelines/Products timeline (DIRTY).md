# General info
The **Cultural Assets** HeriShare deals in are:
## 1. Items of cultural value (inspiration)
### Historical and documentary objects
- Letters, diaries, notebooks, and manuscripts
- First editions and annotated books
- Historical maps, architectural plans, and technical drawings
- Political documents, proclamations, treaties, and campaign materials
- Early photographs, negatives, film reels, and audio recordings
- Newspaper archives and printing plates
- Personal effects belonging to historical figures
- Scientific notebooks, prototypes, and instruments
- Exploration journals and expedition equipment
### Music and performance
- Original musical instruments used by notable performers
- Handwritten scores and lyric sheets
- Stage costumes
- Concert posters and tour memorabilia
- Master recordings and original recording equipment
- Props and scenery from famous theatre productions
- Masks, puppets, and performance objects
- Awards, set lists, backstage passes, and signed contracts
### Film, television, and popular culture
- Film costumes
- Screen-used props
- Miniatures and practical-effects models
- Original animation cells and concept art
- Scripts containing actors’ or directors’ notes
- Cameras or equipment used in historically important productions
- Promotional materials from influential films
- Objects associated with early television, radio, or gaming
- Original arcade machines, consoles, or game-development hardware
### Fashion and design
- Historically important garments
- Couture dresses and runway pieces
- Jewellery associated with public figures
- Designer furniture
- Ceramics, glassware, and industrial-design prototypes
- Original fashion sketches and patterns
- Watches with historical provenance
- Iconic advertising signs and packaging
- Early examples of influential consumer products
### Industrial and technological heritage
- Early computers
- Telecommunications equipment
- Scientific instruments
- Aviation components
- Historic cameras
- Mechanical calculators
- Early robots
- Space-program artefacts
- Manufacturing prototypes
- Objects from closed factories or obsolete industries
### Sports heritage
- Match-worn uniforms
- Championship equipment
- Signed balls, bats, rackets, or gloves
- Olympic torches and medals
- Historic trophies
- Original stadium signage
- Racing vehicles or vehicle components
- Equipment connected to famous sporting moments
- Club archives and founding documents
### Religious and community heritage
- Ceremonial objects
- Historic vestments and textiles
- Community banners
- Processional objects
- Manuscripts and prayer books
- Decorative architectural elements from closed religious buildings
- Objects used in local festivals or community traditions
### Archaeological and ethnographic material
- Coins
- Ceramics
- Tools and domestic objects
- Historic textiles
- Navigational instruments
- Excavated architectural fragments
- Folk costumes
- Traditional craft objects
- Masks and ritual objects
### Architectural heritage
- Stained-glass windows
- Decorative doors and gates
- Sculpted façades
- Mosaics
- Historic fireplaces
- Tiles and architectural ceramics
- Shopfronts and commercial signs
- Interior elements removed from threatened buildings
- Scale models of notable buildings
### Transportation
- Vintage cars
- Historic motorcycles
- Railway carriages and locomotive components
- Boats
- Aircraft
- Bicycles connected to important events
- Public-transport vehicles or signage
### Natural-history and scientific collections
- Fossils
- Meteorites
- Mineral collections
- Taxidermy specimens
- Historic botanical collections
- Anatomical models
- Early medical equipment
- Scientific teaching models
### Local and everyday heritage
- Signs from historic local businesses
- Equipment from a closed cinema, factory, or railway station
- Objects from famous restaurants, clubs, or music venues
- Traditional agricultural tools
- Historic household interiors
- Local newspapers and photographic archives
- Carnival costumes and parade objects
- Community sports-club memorabilia
- Objects recovered from demolished neighbourhood landmarks
## 2. Where the items could come from
### Cultural and public institutions
- Libraries
- Archives
- Universities
- Schools
- Historical societies
- Municipal collections
- Religious institutions
- Theatres, opera houses, and orchestras
- Film institutes
- Cultural centres
- Botanical gardens
- Scientific institutes
- Public broadcasters

They might list objects to finance restoration, digitisation, storage, or continued public access.
### Private and family sources
- Private collectors
- Artist estates
- Families of public figures
- Inherited family collections
- Retired performers or athletes
- Heirs who cannot afford conservation or inheritance-related costs
- Owners of historic homes
- Private foundations

An estate may own culturally significant material but lack the expertise or money to preserve it.
### Commercial sources
- Galleries
- Auction houses
- Antique dealers
- Film studios
- Record labels
- Fashion houses
- Sports clubs
- Publishers
- Hotels and restaurants with historic interiors
- Companies maintaining corporate archives
- Closed factories
- Bankrupt cultural businesses
- Production companies liquidating stored props and costumes

Auction houses could become important after HeriShare’s pivot, when private sellers replace museums and NGOs as the dominant suppliers.
### Community and nonprofit sources
- Heritage charities
- Preservation foundations
- Local-history groups
- Community associations
- Indigenous or diaspora cultural organisations
- Memorial foundations
- Festival organisations
- Historic preservation campaigns
- Volunteer-run cultural centres
- Restoration NGOs

These sources fit HeriShare’s original public-interest identity particularly well.
### Government and public-sector sources

- Municipal governments
- National heritage bodies
- Police or customs agencies disposing of legally forfeited objects
- Decommissioned government departments
- Embassies
- Military archives
- Public transport authorities
- State-owned companies
### Distressed or transitional sources
- Bankrupt institutions
- Closing theatres or cinemas
- Dissolved foundations
- Abandoned estates
- Companies undergoing liquidation
- Institutions damaged by fire, flooding, or conflict
- Organisations unable to pay storage or restoration costs
- Owners facing tax or debt problems
- Collections caught in inheritance disputes

These sources create urgency and explain why owners would accept an unconventional financing arrangement.
### Creators themselves
- Living artists
- Designers
- Photographers
- Writers
- Musicians
- Independent filmmakers
- Craftspeople
- Inventors

A creator might fractionalise an important work without selling it outright, using the funds to finance a future project.
# Phase 1 - Startup
## 9 Feb 2023 - first Prototype
a few mock entries (way beyond scope at that point) to showcase potential
## 11 Apr 2023 - first Partnership
a few artpieces provided by the Bay Area Museum of Art and Design:
1. The Orlov Modular Chair Series, 1974
2. “Signal Loom,” 1982
3. The Van Acker Ceramic Prototype Set, 1968
4. The Luma-3 Exhibition Lamp, 1979
5. “Blue Factory Window,” 1934 / acquired 1978
the museum is very small and unknown, struggling with raising funds for self-maintenance
## 25 Aug 2023 - Major Partnership deal closed
burst of new assets added, from small to mid-sized sources
2-3 more smaller bursts happen after, until 19 Nov 2023

## 1 Sep 2023 - Accelerator Demo Day
a few more entries added thanks to interested parties in the audience (private estate reps, etc)
## 19 Nov 2023 - Press Coverage
after the big influx of new accounts, the media presence also attracts new clients in. 

Steady additions of products in bursts continue, slightly increasing in scope and value over time
## 11 May 2024 - Media Leak
- Multiple NGOs and museums freeze collaboration, threatening legal action
- After this moment, high-status clients come in less and less, bringing in less and less assets of large value

## 9 June 2024 - Internal Breakdown
HeriShare fully freezes the platform in order to fix the legal issue. No more new assets coming in.

## 29 Sep 2024 - Quiet Rebranding
From now on, no new assets from reputable sources. The platform is used to buy and sell valuable assets, keeping/moving them to the private sphere instead of the public one.
